# How to Use Touch Gestures in a Mobile App

## Intro

Taps and typing cover most of a mobile app, but not all of it. A signature pad, a slider, a map, a photo you zoom
into, a list item you hold to see more options: they all need a gesture. A gesture is a path: where the finger touches
the screen, how it moves, how long it takes, and when it lifts.

## A Solution

Describe the gesture as the steps of a finger, and check what it changed on the screen.

- **Appium** sends gestures as W3C actions: a pointer of the touch kind, and a list of moves, presses and releases,
  each with a position and a duration. A long press is a press, a pause, and a release. A pinch is two fingers that
  move at the same time (two pointers).
- **Mobilewright** has `swipe` on a locator or on the screen, `longPress`, and `screen.gesture()` for a path with one
  or more fingers.
- **Espresso** has `swipeLeft()`, `swipeRight()`, `swipeUp()`, `swipeDown()` and `longClick()`, and
  `GeneralSwipeAction` for other paths.
- **XCUITest** has `press(forDuration:thenDragTo:)` on a coordinate, `press(forDuration:)` for a long press, and
  `pinch(withScale:velocity:)`.

The examples use the Drawing screen of Sauce Labs' demo app ("My Demo App", for
[Android](https://github.com/saucelabs/my-demo-app-android) and [iOS](https://github.com/saucelabs/my-demo-app-ios)), a
pad that draws where the finger goes. Each one drags a finger across the pad to draw a line. The drawing is not in the
accessibility tree (it is pixels, not an element), so the test takes a screenshot of the pad before and after the
gesture, and checks that they are different.

On Android, the Drawing screen asks for access to the photos of the device when it opens. The examples grant it up
front (Appium's `autoGrantPermissions`, and Espresso's `GrantPermissionRule`). A later tip covers permission pop-ups.

## Expected Behavior

- Sauce Labs finds a free real device and installs the demo app
- The test opens the Drawing screen from the menu
- It takes a screenshot of the pad, drags a finger across it, and takes another one
- It checks that the two screenshots are different: the gesture drew a line

## Summary

A gesture is a path of a finger: write it as such, and check what it changed. When the change is not in the
accessibility tree, a screenshot of the element is an easy way to see it. For a stricter check, compare it to a
screenshot you saved before (see [tip 73](/tips/73-compare-screenshots) for the web).

Happy Testing!
