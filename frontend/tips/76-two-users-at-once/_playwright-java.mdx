## Code Walkthrough

### Importing Libraries

Lines 3 to 10 pull in the Playwright assertions (`assertThat`), Playwright, its JUnit integration (`@UsePlaywright`),
`UUID` (for the room name), and JUnit.

### One Browser Context for Each User

Lines 15 and 16 build the URL of the chat with a random room. The `join` method (lines 18 to 27) creates a browser
context with `browser.newContext()`: a fresh browser profile, with its own cookies and storage. Two contexts in the
same browser work like two browsers, and they start much faster. Then it opens the chat, waits until the page is
connected (`hasText` waits for it), types the name, clicks "Join", and returns the page of the user.

The `sendMessage` method (lines 29 to 32) types the message and clicks "Send", on the page of the user.

### The Test

`@UsePlaywright` gives the test a `Browser` parameter, to create the contexts. Alice and Bob join (lines 36 and 37).
Messages are not saved, so Alice waits until she sees "Bob joined" before she writes (line 39). Then Alice sends "Hi
Bob", and Bob's page should show it (lines 41 and 42), and the other way around (lines 44 and 45). `containsText`
waits until the text shows up, and fails the test when it doesn't. At the end, the test closes both contexts.
