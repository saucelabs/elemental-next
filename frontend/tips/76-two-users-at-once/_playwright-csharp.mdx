## Code Walkthrough

### Importing Libraries

Lines 1 to 5 pull in `System` (for `Guid`), Playwright, its MSTest integration, and MSTest.

### One Browser Context for Each User

Lines 13 and 14 build the URL of the chat with a random room. The test class extends `BrowserTest`, which gives it a
`Browser`. The `Join` method (lines 16 to 26) creates a browser context with `Browser.NewContextAsync()`: a fresh
browser profile, with its own cookies and storage. Two contexts in the same browser work like two browsers, and they
start much faster. Then it opens the chat, waits until the page is connected (`ToHaveTextAsync` waits for it), types
the name, clicks "Join", and returns the page of the user.

The `SendMessage` method (lines 28 to 32) types the message and clicks "Send", on the page of the user.

### The Test

Alice and Bob join (lines 37 and 38). Messages are not saved, so Alice waits until she sees "Bob joined" before she
writes (line 40). Then Alice sends "Hi Bob", and Bob's page should show it (lines 42 and 43), and the other way around
(lines 45 and 46). `ToContainTextAsync` waits until the text shows up, and fails the test when it doesn't. At the end,
the test closes both contexts.
