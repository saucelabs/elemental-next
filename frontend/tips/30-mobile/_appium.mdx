## Code Walkthrough

### Configuring Sauce Labs

In WebdriverIO, the devices are part of the configuration, not of the test.

<TipCode codePath={"30-mobile/code/appium/wdio.conf.js"} language={"js"}/>

- Lines 3 to 5 give WebdriverIO the Sauce Labs user name and access key (from the `SAUCE_USERNAME` and
  `SAUCE_ACCESS_KEY` environment variables) and the region of the data center (`us` is `us-west-1`).
- Lines 6 to 9 turn on the Sauce service (the `@wdio/sauce-service` package). It names each job on Sauce Labs and
  marks it as passed or failed when the test ends.
- Lines 12 to 24 ask for two devices, and the test runs on each one. The Android one (lines 13 to 17) uses the
  `UiAutomator2` Appium driver, and the iPhone one (lines 19 to 23) the `XCUITest` driver. The device name is a regular
  expression (`Google.*` is any Google phone), and the test gets the first free device that matches. `appium:app` is
  the demo app from its GitHub release: Sauce Labs downloads it and installs it on the device.
- Line 11 runs one device at a time, and lines 25 and 26 give Sauce Labs up to 5 minutes to find a device and install
  the app.
- Line 27 makes the assertions wait up to 30 seconds. The app shows a splash screen first, so the catalog is not there
  right away.
- Lines 32 to 37 skip the run when the Sauce Labs credentials are not set.

### The Test

The test (lines 4 to 9) looks for the backpack and checks that it is displayed, waiting for it if needed. On Android
(line 6), it uses a UiAutomator selector with the text "Sauce Labs Backpack". On iOS, the product name is
"Sauce Labs Backpack - Black", and line 7 finds it with an iOS predicate on its label. `driver.isAndroid` tells which
device the test is running on.
