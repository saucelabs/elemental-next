# How to Run a Test on a Real Mobile Device

## Intro

Your users run your app on real phones: many brands and models, different OS versions, screen sizes, and the
manufacturers' own changes to Android. Emulators and simulators are great while you write the app, but they don't show
how it behaves on the phones your users have. Buying and maintaining a shelf of phones to test on is slow and costly.

## A Solution

Run your tests on real devices in the cloud. [Sauce Labs](https://saucelabs.com/) has a cloud of real Android
phones and iPhones. Your test asks for a device (e.g., any Google Pixel, or any iPhone), Sauce Labs installs your app on
it, and the test drives the app, as it would on a phone on your desk. Each test run records a video, screenshots and the
device logs.

Every example here uses Sauce Labs' demo app ("My Demo App", for
[Android](https://github.com/saucelabs/my-demo-app-android) and [iOS](https://github.com/saucelabs/my-demo-app-ios)),
a small shop app. The test opens the app and checks that the catalog shows the Sauce Labs Backpack. Sauce Labs
downloads the app from its GitHub release, so you don't need to build or upload anything to try it.

There are two kinds of mobile test tools:

- **Appium** and **Mobilewright** drive the app from outside, like a user would. One test can run on Android and iOS,
  and you can write it in many languages. The test runs on your machine and talks to the device over the network.
- **Espresso** (Android) and **XCUITest** (iOS) are the test frameworks from Google and Apple. The tests are written in
  the language of the app (Kotlin and Swift), built into a test app, and run on the device itself. Sauce Labs'
  [saucectl](https://docs.saucelabs.com/dev/cli/saucectl/) uploads the app and the tests and starts the run.

## Example

__NOTE:__ You'll need an account to use Sauce Labs. You can sign up for a
[free trial account (no credit-card required)](https://saucelabs.com/sign-up).

Export your Sauce Labs username and access key as the environment variables `SAUCE_USERNAME` and `SAUCE_ACCESS_KEY`,
instead of hard-coding them in your tests. If they are not set, the tests are skipped.

The examples use the `us-west-1` data center. Each one asks for a device by a pattern of its name (e.g., `Google.*` or
`iPhone.*`), so any free device that matches can run the test.

## Expected Behavior

- Sauce Labs finds a free real device that matches the test (an Android phone, an iPhone, or both)
- It installs the demo app (and, for Espresso and XCUITest, the tests)
- The test opens the app and checks that the catalog shows the Sauce Labs Backpack
- The session ends, and the results (with a video, screenshots and the device logs) are available on the
  [test results dashboard](https://docs.saucelabs.com/test-results/viewing-test-results/)

## Summary

With a test running on a real device, you can check your app on the phones your users have, without keeping any of
them on your desk. The next tips build on this one: installing your own app, finding elements, waiting for them, and
answering permission pop-ups.

Happy Testing!
