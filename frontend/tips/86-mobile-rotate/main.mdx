# How to Rotate a Mobile Device in a Test

## Intro

People turn their phones to watch a video, to type with both thumbs, or by accident. When the device rotates, the app
lays out its screen again: elements move, lists show more columns, and things that fit in portrait can end up hidden.
On Android, the screen is even created again, and an app that doesn't save its state loses what the user typed.

## A Solution

Rotate the device in the test, and check the screen in landscape, as you do in portrait:

- **Appium** has `rotate` and `getOrientation` in Java, the `orientation` property in Python, and `setOrientation` and
  `getOrientation` in WebdriverIO, with `PORTRAIT` and `LANDSCAPE`.
- **Mobilewright** has `device.setOrientation()` and `device.getOrientation()`, with `portrait` and `landscape`.
- **Espresso** runs inside the app, so it rotates the screen of the app: it sets the requested orientation of the
  activity. UI Automator's `UiDevice.setOrientationLeft()` rotates the whole device instead.
- **XCUITest** sets `XCUIDevice.shared.orientation`, the orientation of the device.

An app can lock its orientation. The Android version of Sauce Labs' demo app ("My Demo App") runs in portrait only (its
manifest sets `android:screenOrientation="portrait"`), so rotating the device doesn't change it: Appium fails ("Screen
rotation cannot be changed"), and Mobilewright reports that the device is still in portrait. So the Appium and
Mobilewright examples rotate an iPhone, with the [iOS demo app](https://github.com/saucelabs/my-demo-app-ios). The
Espresso example rotates the Android app from inside, which overrides its manifest, as the
[Android demo app](https://github.com/saucelabs/my-demo-app-android) does in its own tests.

Each example waits for the catalog, rotates to landscape, checks the orientation and that the catalog still shows its
products, and rotates back. Rotation tests find real layout issues: in landscape, the tab bar of the iOS demo app covers
the names of the products. That's why the tests check the products themselves, which stay visible.

## Expected Behavior

- Sauce Labs finds a free real device and installs the demo app
- The test waits for the catalog, in portrait
- It rotates to landscape, and checks the orientation and that the products are visible
- It rotates back to portrait (Appium, Mobilewright and XCUITest)

## Summary

A rotation is a cheap check that catches layout bugs and lost state. Add it to the screens where users are likely to
turn their phones, and check what they would look at.

Happy Testing!
